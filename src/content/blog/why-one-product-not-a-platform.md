Every early-stage software company eventually faces the same temptation: sketch out the whole platform now, while the whiteboard is still clean. It's a satisfying exercise, and it's usually a mistake. We've chosen not to, on purpose, from the start.

## The problem with building breadth before depth

A roadmap full of future products is easy to draw and expensive to promise. It's also backwards — it puts ambition ahead of evidence. We'd rather prove one thing works, with real people, than sketch six things that might.

## What we're actually doing instead

Right now, Tixible builds exactly one product: Closure, a daily decision companion for small business owners. It's in concept validation — shaped through direct conversations with the business owners it's meant for, not from a feature list we wrote in isolation.

## The principle underneath it

Earn the right to expand. We won't build a second product, a second feature set, or a platform narrative until Closure has actually earned it — through real field evidence, real retention, and real validation, in that order. Ambition without evidence is just a nicer word for guessing.
